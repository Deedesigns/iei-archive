## From UFO to UAP

![Grey infrared frame from a Navy targeting pod showing a dark blurred object above clouds](gimbal.jpg)
*Frame from the "Gimbal" video, recorded by U.S. Navy aircrew in 2015 and officially released by the Department of Defense in April 2020. Credit: U.S. Navy, via [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gimbal_The_First_Official_UAP_Footage_from_the_USG_for_Public_Release.webm), public domain.*

The U.S. government now uses the term **unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP)**. Interest rose after The New York Times reported in 2017 on a Pentagon program that studied the topic, and the Navy later acknowledged videos recorded by fighter pilots, often called "Tic Tac", "Gimbal" and "GoFast".

## The 2021 preliminary assessment

![Horizontal bar chart: 1 report explained, 143 unexplained](odni-2021.png)
*Of 144 reports reviewed, the 2021 assessment explained one (a deflating balloon). Chart: IEI. Data: [ODNI Preliminary Assessment, June 25, 2021](https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/Prelimary-Assessment-UAP-20210625.pdf).*

In June 2021 the Office of the Director of National Intelligence published a preliminary assessment of 144 reports from U.S. government sources between 2004 and 2021. It was able to explain only one, as a deflating balloon, and said most reports probably represented physical objects but that data was too limited to determine what they were.

## AARO

Congress then created the **All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO)** in the Department of Defense. AARO publishes annual reports, and many cases it resolves turn out to be balloons, drones, birds, satellites or sensor artifacts.

## The 2024 historical review

In March 2024 AARO released Volume I of its historical record report, reviewing U.S. government involvement with UAP since 1945. It found **no evidence** that any government investigation, research or program had confirmed extraterrestrial technology, and no evidence of a hidden reverse-engineering program. The report also said many sightings were misidentified classified aircraft or ordinary objects.

## Disputed testimony

In July 2023, former intelligence officer David Grusch testified to a House subcommittee that the U.S. had a secret program to retrieve non-human craft. He did not present public evidence, and AARO's review did not support the claims. They remain disputed.
