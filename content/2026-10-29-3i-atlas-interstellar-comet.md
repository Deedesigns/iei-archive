## A visitor from another star

On July 1, 2025, the NASA-funded ATLAS survey telescope at Río Hurtado, Chile, reported a faint object moving far too fast to be bound to the Sun. Within a day the Minor Planet Center gave it the name **3I/ATLAS** (also C/2025 N1): the third confirmed interstellar object, after 1I/'Oumuamua in 2017 (see our ['Oumuamua page](https://deedesigns.github.io/iei-archive/articles/oumuamua-alien-probe.html)) and 2I/Borisov in 2019. Exactly one year ago today, on **October 29, 2025**, it made its closest approach to the Sun at about 1.36 AU (roughly 203 million km), between the orbits of Earth and Mars. It never came closer to Earth than about 1.8 AU (around 270 million km) and posed no threat.

## Why it was exciting

- **Speed.** It entered the solar system at about 58 km/s relative to the Sun, far faster than 'Oumuamua (about 26 km/s) or Borisov (about 32 km/s).
- **Shape of its path.** Its orbital eccentricity is about 6.14. Anything above 1 is unbound; this path is so open it is almost a straight line.
- **Age.** Its motion through the galaxy suggests it may belong to the Milky Way's older "thick disk". A 2025 study by Matthew Hopkins and colleagues estimated an age of 7.6 to 14 billion years (68% confidence), which would make it older than our 4.6-billion-year-old solar system. That is an estimate from its velocity, not a measurement of the comet itself.

## The claim: alien technology?

On July 16, 2025, Adam Hibberd, Adam Crowl and Harvard astrophysicist **Avi Loeb** posted a paper on arXiv titled *"Is the Interstellar Object 3I/ATLAS Alien Technology?"* The authors described it as "largely a pedagogical exercise," but argued that several features were anomalous:

- Its path lies close to the plane of the planets (tilted about 5 degrees, moving retrograde) and passes fairly near Venus, Mars and Jupiter, which they calculated as a probability of 0.005% or less.
- It would be hidden behind the Sun, as seen from Earth, around perihelion, which they suggested could allow a concealed braking manoeuvre.
- Early size estimates looked large for an interstellar object, and at first no gas had been identified.

Crucially, the paper made **testable predictions**: an optimal intercept of Earth would mean an arrival in late November or early December 2025, and a particular non-gravitational acceleration would signal an intent to rendezvous with Jupiter.

## What the evidence shows

Few comets have ever been watched so closely. Hubble, Webb, SPHEREx, TESS, Swift, the Very Large Telescope, Mars orbiters and rovers, Europa Clipper, Psyche, Lucy, Parker Solar Probe, PUNCH, SOHO and ESA's Juice all observed it.

- **It was a comet from the first week.** Telescopes on July 2, 2025 already saw a fuzzy coma; David Jewitt and Jane Luu reported it was "clearly active." TESS images found it active as far back as May 2025.
- **It outgasses ordinary comet chemistry.** Webb found a coma dominated by carbon dioxide, with water, carbon monoxide and carbonyl sulfide (Cordiner et al. 2025). The VLT detected cyanide and nickel vapour at levels similar to Solar System comets, and Webb later added methane. The high CO2 ratio is unusual, but its proposed explanations are about where and how the comet formed.
- **It is small.** Hubble put the nucleus between 0.32 and 5.6 km wide, likely at the low end. Spacecraft-based measurements of its non-gravitational push, which is what outgassing jets produce, imply a nucleus roughly 0.5 to 0.75 km across.
- **It grew tails.** A Sun-facing dust plume and then an anti-solar dust tail developed as it warmed, and ESA reported a gas tail millions of kilometres long.
- **The predictions did not come true.** It passed Earth at about 1.8 AU on December 19, 2025, flew 0.36 AU from Jupiter on March 16, 2026 without stopping, and is on its way out of the solar system.
- **The statistics were questioned.** Astronomers including Chris Lintott, Scott Manley and David Kipping publicly criticised the trajectory analysis; an alignment with the planets' plane is unlikely for any one object but is not evidence of steering.

Loeb himself wrote that the most likely outcome was a natural object, probably a comet, and treated the alien idea as a thought experiment.

## Verdict

**Explained.** 3I/ATLAS is a natural interstellar comet. It showed a coma, tails and normal cometary gases from discovery onward, its non-gravitational acceleration is the size expected from outgassing, and the specific spacecraft predictions (an Earth intercept, a Jupiter rendezvous) failed. What remains genuinely open is scientific, not alien: why it is so rich in CO2 and so ancient-looking. That is a real gift, since it is a sample of how planets formed around another star, possibly billions of years before the Sun existed.
