## The most-watched UFO hearing in decades

![Three witnesses seated at a long wooden table in a House hearing room, with name plates reading Mr. Graves, Mr. Grusch and CDR. Fravor (Ret.), and a crowded gallery seated behind them](grusch-hearing-panel.jpg)
*Ryan Graves, David Grusch and retired Navy Commander David Fravor at the witness table on July 26, 2023. Credit: U.S. House Committee on Oversight and Accountability (still from the official hearing video), via [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:US_congressional_hearing_on_UAPs_on_26_July_2023_with_Grusch_Graves_and_Fravor.png), public domain.*

On the morning of **Wednesday, July 26, 2023**, in room 2154 of the Rayburn House Office Building, the House Oversight Subcommittee on National Security, the Border, and Foreign Affairs held a hearing titled **"Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Implications on National Security, Public Safety, and Government Transparency."** Chairman Glenn Grothman opened it by calling it "the most exciting Subcommittee in Congress this week."

Three witnesses were sworn in:

- **Ryan Graves**, a former Navy F/A-18 pilot and executive director of Americans for Safe Aerospace, who described objects he and his squadron detected off the U.S. East Coast in 2014 and 2015.
- **Commander David Fravor (Ret.)**, who described his 2004 "Tic Tac" encounter off San Diego (see our full page on [the Nimitz encounter](nimitz-tic-tac-2004.html)).
- **David Grusch**, a former Air Force intelligence officer and National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA) official. His claims are the reason the hearing became front-page news, and they are the focus of this page.

Graves's and Fravor's testimony was about things they or colleagues say they saw. Grusch's was different: it was about what he says **other people told him**. Keeping that distinction in mind is the key to understanding the whole episode.

## Who is David Grusch?

According to his written statement and the résumé he filed with the committee, Grusch spent 14 years as an intelligence officer. He served on active duty in the Air Force, then as an Air Force Reserve officer at the National Reconnaissance Office (NRO), where he says he was a member of the UAP Task Force from 2019 to 2021. From late 2021 to April 2023 he was a GS-15 civilian at the NGA, which he describes as the equivalent of a "full-bird Colonel," and his agency's co-lead for UAP analysis. He holds a physics degree from the University of Pittsburgh. By the time of the hearing he was chief operating officer of a nonprofit, The Sol Foundation.

None of that is in serious dispute. These are real, senior-sounding credentials. But credentials tell you a person was in a position to *hear* things. They don't tell you whether what they heard was true.

## How the story broke

Grusch went public on **June 5, 2023**, in an article by Leslie Kean and Ralph Blumenthal on the news site *The Debrief*, "Intelligence Officials Say U.S. Has Retrieved Craft of Non-Human Origin." (Kean and Blumenthal, with Helene Cooper, had co-written the 2017 *New York Times* story that revealed the Pentagon's AATIP program.) The article reported that:

- In **May 2022**, Grusch's lawyer, Charles McCullough III, the first Inspector General of the Intelligence Community, filed a "Disclosure of Urgent Concern(s); Complaint of Reprisal" with the Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG).
- The ICIG found the complaint **"credible and urgent"** in July 2022, according to the article.
- Grusch said recovered objects were "of exotic origin (non-human intelligence, whether extraterrestrial or unknown origin) based on the vehicle morphologies and material science testing and the possession of unique atomic arrangements and radiological signatures."
- The Pentagon's prepublication review office had cleared his on-the-record statements "for open publication" in April 2023.
- And, importantly: "Congress has not been provided with any physical materials related to wreckage or other non-human objects."

Two of those points are often misread, so it's worth being precise.

**"Credible and urgent"** is a term from the whistleblower process for intelligence employees. It means the Inspector General found the complaint serious enough, and plausible enough on its face, to be forwarded to Congress's intelligence committees. It is **not** a finding that the underlying allegations about alien craft are true.

**"Cleared for open publication"** means the Pentagon's security reviewers decided the statements didn't reveal classified information. It is **not** an endorsement of their accuracy. Grusch said as much at the hearing: his testimony was limited to what that security review had determined was unclassified.

## What Grusch said under oath

Here is what is on the official hearing transcript (Serial No. 118-53), in his own words where possible.

**The core claim.** In his opening statement: "I was informed in the course of my official duties of a multi-decade UAP crash retrieval and reverse engineering program to which I was denied access to those additional read-ons when I requested it." His testimony, he said, "is based on information I have been given by individuals with a long-standing track record of legitimacy," many of whom "shared compelling evidence in the form of photography, official documentation, and classified oral testimony." He also said: "I am speaking to the facts as I have been told them."

**Possession.** Asked by Rep. Robert Garcia whether the government possesses UAPs: "Absolutely, based on interviewing over 40 witnesses over 4 years." He said he knew "the exact locations," and had given them to the Inspector General and some to the intelligence committees.

**Since when.** Asked by Rep. Tim Burchett when the government first became aware of "nonhuman" intelligence, he said, "Certainly, like I have discussed publicly, previously 1930's." He added, "I like to use the term nonhuman. I do not like to denote origin."

**Bodies.** Asked by Rep. Nancy Mace about pilots of crashed craft: "biologics came with some of these recoveries." Human or nonhuman? "Nonhuman, and that was the assessment of people with direct knowledge on the program I talked to that are currently still on the program."

**What he saw himself.** This exchange with Rep. Eric Burlison is the one to remember:

- *Have you seen the spacecraft?* "I have to be careful to describe what I have seen firsthand and not in this environment. But I could answer that question behind closed doors."
- *Have you seen any of the bodies?* "That is something I have not witnessed myself."

**Money and harm.** Asked how such a program could be funded, he said "misappropriation of funds and self-fund," and that he had "specific knowledge" of money diverted from its budgeted purpose. Asked whether he personally knew of people harmed in efforts to cover up the program, he answered "Yes, personally," and said he had "directed people with that knowledge to the appropriate authorities." He described "very brutal" reprisals against himself, citing an open whistleblower reprisal investigation.

**What he wouldn't say.** Over and over, the most specific questions got the same kind of answer. Program names, facilities, documentary evidence and crash imagery were things he said he could discuss only "in a SCIF" (a secure facility for classified material) or "in a closed session." Members complained that they had been denied a classified briefing with him before the hearing. That is a real constraint on what he could say in public. It also means **no evidence was presented at the hearing** that the public, or reporters, could examine.

## The Pentagon's answer

The Defense Department responded in an emailed statement from spokesperson Susan Gough, first issued in early June 2023 and quoted widely around the hearing: "To date, AARO has not discovered any verifiable information to substantiate claims that any programs regarding the possession or reverse-engineering of extraterrestrial materials have existed in the past or exist currently."

Note what that sentence does and doesn't cover. It speaks for AARO, the Pentagon's UAP office, and it says "extraterrestrial," while Grusch deliberately said "non-human" and declined to "denote origin." Supporters argued this wording left room. The fuller answer came eight months later.

## AARO's historical review, March 2024

Congress, in the 2023 defense bill, ordered AARO to review the entire U.S. government record on UAP. **Volume I of its Historical Record Report** was released on March 8, 2024. AARO says it reviewed every official investigation since 1945, searched classified and unclassified archives, conducted about 30 interviews, and worked with the officials who oversee classified special-access programs. Its main conclusions:

- "AARO found no empirical evidence for claims that the USG and private companies have been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology." Claims "involving specific people, known locations, technological tests, and documents" were judged "inaccurate."
- In many cases interviewees had named **real** classified programs, but "mistakenly associated these authentic USG programs with alien and extraterrestrial activity." In one example, a claim that an officer had touched an off-world craft traced back, according to the officer, to a story about touching an **F-117 stealth fighter**.
- AARO assessed that the reverse-engineering claim "is, in large part, the result of **circular reporting** from a group of individuals who believe this to be the case, despite the lack of any evidence," a group it said had been involved in UAP efforts "since at least 2009."
- It described **KONA BLUE**, a proposed UAP recovery and reverse-engineering program pitched to the Department of Homeland Security, which "was never approved" and whose supporters "never provided empirical evidence."
- It also said that in 2021 an intelligence-community classified program's scope had been expanded "without sufficient justification" to cover UAP reverse-engineering. AARO says that program was later disestablished for lack of merit, and that it had not recovered or reverse-engineered any UAP or extraterrestrial craft.

The report doesn't name Grusch as the source of any specific claim, and it is not the last word: AARO said "additional claims will be addressed in Volume II." Critics, including many in Congress, have argued that the Pentagon is effectively investigating itself. That criticism is fair to note. But it cuts both ways: the review is the only systematic, on-the-record examination of these claims, and on the specific claims it checked, the answer was no.

## What Congress did next

The hearing fed directly into legislation. In July 2023 Senators Chuck Schumer and Mike Rounds proposed the **UAP Disclosure Act**, modeled on the 1992 JFK assassination records law. It would have created an independent presidentially appointed Review Board and required the government to exercise **eminent domain** over any recovered "non-human" material held by private companies.

The version that became law in the **Fiscal Year 2024 National Defense Authorization Act (Sections 1841–1843)** was much narrower. According to the conference report, the final agreement kept only the requirement for a government-wide **UAP Records Collection** at the National Archives and a process for reviewing records for release. It dropped the independent Review Board, its staff, the eminent-domain power and the "controlled disclosure" plan. The National Archives has since created **Record Group 615** for these records.

That outcome is consistent with two different stories, and honesty requires saying so. Supporters see it as evidence that someone powerful didn't want the full act to pass. Skeptics see it as Congress declining to legislate around a claim no one had substantiated. The bill's history alone can't tell you which is right.

## Evidence, claims and speculation

- **Evidence (solid):** The hearing happened and the transcript is public. Grusch's career and credentials are documented in his own filings. His ICIG complaint was filed in May 2022 and, per his lawyers and *The Debrief*, judged "credible and urgent." He testified under oath that he was told of a crash-retrieval program and was denied access to it.
- **Evidence (against the core claim):** AARO's 2024 review, with access to special-access program oversight, found no empirical evidence of reverse-engineering programs and judged the specific claims it examined inaccurate. No physical material, document, photo or named program has been made public, and *The Debrief* itself reported that none had been given to Congress.
- **Claims (unverified):** That the U.S. has recovered intact non-human craft since the 1930s; that "nonhuman biologics" were recovered; that funds were misappropriated to hide it; that people were harmed to keep it secret. All of these rest on Grusch's account of what others told him, and he said under oath that he had not seen the bodies himself.
- **Speculation:** Whether classified material exists that would change the picture if it were released. That can't be ruled out by anyone outside the classified system, but it also can't be counted as evidence.

## Evidence verdict

**Disputed.** David Grusch is a credentialed former official who testified under oath, and there's no public evidence he lied about what he was told. But the central claim, that the U.S. holds and reverse-engineers non-human craft, is **secondhand testimony with no public evidence behind it**, and the government's only formal review of these claims rejected them. AARO's finding that real secret programs have been mistaken for alien ones offers an ordinary explanation that fits the pattern. It isn't proven to explain every claim, so we stop short of "Explained." The test is simple: if the documents, photos or material Grusch describes exist, they could be shown to Congress and, eventually, to the public. Until then, extraordinary claims still need extraordinary evidence, and this one doesn't have it yet.
