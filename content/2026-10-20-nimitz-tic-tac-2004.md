If one modern UFO case gets called "the best," it's this one. In November 2004 the **USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group** was training off Southern California and Baja California. Before it deployed, aircrew reported a smooth white object shaped like a breath mint. It seemed to outfly their brand-new fighters. Thirteen years later, The New York Times put the story on its front page. Then the Pentagon officially released the infrared video. And in 2023, the lead pilot repeated his account under oath to Congress.

Below we sort what is **documented**, what is **testimony**, and what is **speculation**. The case deserves real curiosity, and also real care.

## What the witnesses say happened

The most detailed first-person account is **Commander David Fravor's** written statement to the House Oversight subcommittee hearing of **July 26, 2023**. Fravor, now retired, commanded Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (the "Black Aces") aboard Nimitz. His account:

- He led two two-seat **F/A-18Fs** on a training exercise controlled by the Aegis cruiser **USS Princeton (CG-59)**. Princeton suspended the training for "real world tasking."
- Princeton's controller said the ship had been watching these objects on its Aegis system "for the previous 2 weeks." In Fravor's words, they "had been descending from above 80,000ft and coming rapidly down to 20,000ft would stay for hours and then go straight back up."
- The jets reached the spot at 20,000 ft and saw "white water" on an otherwise calm sea. Fravor wrote: "As all 4 looked down we saw a small white Tic Tac shaped object". It had "No Rotors, No Rotor wash, or any visible flight control surfaces like wings."
- Fravor spiraled down toward it. He says the object "aligned it with my aircraft and began to climb." When he pulled his nose onto it at about half a mile, "it rapidly accelerated and disappeared right in front of our aircraft."
- Princeton then reported the object back on radar at the jets' combat air patrol point, about **60 miles** away, "in a very short period of time (less than a minute)."
- He also wrote that the video shows the object "does not emit any IR (infrared) plume," and that a radar tape, which has not been released, showed jamming of the jet's APG-73 radar.

His wingman, **Lt. Alex Dietrich**, backed up the core sighting on CBS's *60 Minutes* in 2021. Fravor told the program that "There was four of us in the airplanes literally watching this thing for roughly about five minutes." Dietrich described "No predictable movement, no predictable trajectory." She also gave one of the most candid lines in the whole case: "if I saw this solo, I don't know that I would have come back and said anything." Fravor's own conclusion is careful about *who*: "I don't know who's building it, who's got the technology... But there's... something out there that was better than our airplane."

## The video, and what it does and does not show

Fravor says that after his flight landed, another crew launched and recorded the now-famous clip. The Pentagon's released version runs about **76 seconds**. It switches between infrared and the pod's TV camera and shows a small blob that the pod keeps tracking.

The key caveat comes from the 2009 investigative report described below, as summarized by The War Zone: the crew that filmed it **could not confirm it was the same object** Fravor's flight had seen, because they never saw it with their own eyes. Calling the FLIR1 video "footage of the Tic Tac Fravor saw" is therefore an assumption. The record does not establish it.

The video's official history:

- **December 16, 2017:** The New York Times ran Helene Cooper, Ralph Blumenthal and Leslie Kean's reports, including "2 Navy Airmen and an Object That 'Accelerated Like Nothing I've Ever Seen,'" next to its exposé of a secret Pentagon UFO-study program.
- **April 27, 2020:** The Department of Defense officially released this video and two 2015 videos (GIMBAL and GOFAST). It said they had been "circulating in the public domain after unauthorized releases in 2007 and 2017." It released them "to clear up any misconceptions by the public on whether or not the footage that has been circulating was real." It added: "The aerial phenomena observed in the videos remain characterized as 'unidentified.'"

That last sentence is often read as the Pentagon admitting something extraordinary. It is narrower than that. "Unidentified" means *not identified*. It is not a finding of anomalous performance.

## The "unofficial official report"

In May 2018, Las Vegas station KLAS published a 13-page, undated document with no cover sheet. Fravor calls it the "Unofficial Official Report." According to The War Zone and Fravor's testimony, it came from the Pentagon's AATIP-era study effort and was compiled in **2009**. Its executive summary describes detections over about **November 10–16, 2004**. In those detections, objects it calls "Anomalous Aerial Vehicles" would descend "very rapidly" from "approximately 60,000 feet down to approximately 50 feet in a matter of seconds." It puts the object at "approximately 46 feet in length." It says the F/A-18F radar "could not obtain a 'lock'" while the FLIR pod could track it.

Its "Key Assessments" go much further. It says the object "was no known aircraft or air vehicle currently in the inventory of the United States or any foreign nation." It also says it "possibly demonstrated the ability to 'cloak'" and to operate undersea undetected.

Treat this document carefully. It is an **analysis written five years later**, largely from interviews. Its authorship and official status are murky. And its own numbers don't match the witnesses': 60,000 ft to 50 ft here, versus "above 80,000ft" to 20,000 ft in Fravor's testimony. It is evidence of what investigators were told. It is not raw sensor data.

## The skeptical analyses

**Mick West**, the best-known skeptical analyst of the Navy videos, argues that the FLIR1 clip "is most likely a distant plane." He says the object "doesn't actually move on screen - except when the camera moves," and that it "resembles an out of focus low-resolution backlit plane." His later simulations model the pod's defocus, noise and low resolution to reproduce the blob. He is explicit about the limits of this argument: "I don't know what the pilots saw, but this video does not show anything really interesting." He has also put forward parallax and perception hypotheses for parts of the eyewitness account. Those are hypotheses. None has been confirmed.

Two cautions apply to both sides:

- **The video and the sighting are separate pieces of evidence.** A mundane video does not debunk four eyewitnesses. Four eyewitnesses do not make the video extraordinary.
- **The headline performance numbers come from radar operators, not from data anyone can inspect.** Examples are drops of tens of thousands of feet in seconds and 60 miles in under a minute. Witness estimates of size, distance and speed against open ocean, with no reference points, are notoriously hard. Former AARO deputy director **Tim Phillips** (more below) has noted the "limitations" of human eyes in judging "speed, you know, mass, velocity while you're moving at 300 knots," and former director **Sean Kirkpatrick** agreed that pilots are as vulnerable to optical illusions as anyone.

## What happened to the data

This is the part of the case that frustrates everyone.

In a March 2024 interview recorded for The Guardian (transcript published by Metabunk), Kirkpatrick said: "There's no data, there's not enough information to ever come to a conclusion." He listed non-alien hypotheses he considered not fully explored, "like submersible launched vehicles, other radar artefacts." He said that none of the data "was retained at the time, because there wasn't a data retention policy in place."

In June 2025, former AARO deputy (and acting) director **Tim Phillips** gave more detail in a long interview with Mick West:

- "the Navy had... completed their investigation and shared it with AARO, hasn't gone public yet."
- Third Fleet staff "directed that the radar tapes be preserved." The recordings went to Third Fleet in San Diego and then physically to the Navy lab at **Dahlgren, Virginia**. But "we did two physical searches trying to find that data. We could not find that data." The ships' operational logs for the period could not be found either.
- A long-told story says people in dark jumpsuits came aboard Princeton by helicopter and left with materials. Phillips said AARO traced that to **DARPA personnel** working with the strike group on a new lightweight ISR (surveillance) system, which he believed was a "WASP." He said "the Princeton may have detected that." But he was frank that this is not a resolution: "are we ever going to have the data to understand what happened? Not after 20 years."

As of **October 20, 2026**, AARO's public list of [UAP case resolution reports](https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/UAP-Case-Resolution-Reports/) contains eight resolved cases, including **GoFast** from the same set of Navy videos. **Nimitz is not among them.** The government's 2026 PURSUE program has published six tranches of *unresolved* UAP files (the latest on September 18, 2026). We found no Nimitz-specific record in an independent file-by-file index of those releases. That is a search result, not proof that none exists.

For the wider government picture, see our earlier page on [what the U.S. government has officially said about UAP](https://deedesigns.github.io/iei-archive/articles/us-government-uap-reports.html).

## Claims vs. evidence at a glance

- **Documented:** The FLIR1 video is genuine Navy footage, officially released, and the DoD calls the phenomena "unidentified." Multiple aircrew reported a sighting on November 14, 2004, and the lead pilot repeated it under oath in 2023.
- **Testimony only:** The object's shape, size, "mirroring" behavior, sudden departure, and reappearance 60 miles away. The weeks of radar tracks from 80,000 (or 60,000) ft.
- **Unverified analysis:** The 2009 "executive report" and its claims of cloaking, undersea operation and non-terrestrial origin.
- **Speculation, on every side:** Alien craft, secret U.S. or foreign drones, DARPA test systems, radar artifacts, distant airliners. Without the radar data, none can be confirmed or ruled out for the visual sighting.

## Our verdict: Unexplained

**What is established:** Experienced naval aviators reported seeing something they could not identify, and the Navy recorded an infrared target in the same exercise. The Pentagon confirmed the video is real and still labels the phenomena "unidentified." No one has published a resolution.

**What is not established:** That the object did anything physically impossible, or that it was a craft from anywhere else. The released video, taken on its own, has a plausible mundane reading. The most dramatic performance claims rest on recollections and secondhand radar reports. The radar recordings that could test them were preserved, sent to a Navy lab, and then apparently lost.

So the Nimitz encounter is honestly **Unexplained**: a real, well-witnessed event with too little surviving data to resolve. It is not evidence of extraterrestrial visitors. If the Navy's completed investigation is ever released, or the Dahlgren tapes turn up, we will revisit this page. This page reflects sources we could verify as of October 20, 2026.
