## The planet

K2-18b is a *sub-Neptune*: about 2.6 times Earth's radius and about 8.6 times its mass. It orbits a small red dwarf star (spectral type M2.5V) inside that star's habitable zone. Its bulk properties fit several very different interiors. One is a **"hycean" world**, a proposed planet type with a global water ocean under a hydrogen-rich atmosphere. Others are a gas-rich mini-Neptune or a rocky "gas dwarf" with a thick hydrogen envelope.

## The claim

- **2023:** A team led by Nikku Madhusudhan (University of Cambridge) used JWST's near-infrared instruments (NIRISS and NIRSpec) to report methane and carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. They also reported a weak hint of **dimethyl sulfide (DMS)**, which on Earth is produced by life, mainly marine microbes. The team itself described that hint as low significance.
- **April 2025:** The same group published a mid-infrared spectrum taken with JWST's MIRI instrument in April 2024. They reported "new independent evidence for DMS and/or DMDS" (dimethyl disulfide) at **3-sigma** significance, at abundances of roughly 10 ppm or more. They noted that the two molecules are hard to tell apart in this data. They also wrote that more observations were needed and that abiotic (non-living) sources still had to be studied.

Three sigma is below the 5-sigma bar physicists usually require for a "discovery". The authors themselves said it was "at the lower end of robustness typically required for scientific evidence."

## The pushback

Independent teams reanalyzed the public JWST data over the following months:

- **MIRI data alone:** One analysis asked whether the MIRI spectrum shows any real features at all. It found the data could be fit by a flat line, meaning no features. It also pointed out that the 3-sigma figure came from a curated "canonical" model with only a few molecules. In the team's broader 20-molecule model, a no-DMS/DMDS fit was rejected at less than 2 sigma.
- **Near-infrared data:** A reanalysis that ran 60 data treatments and more than 250 retrievals confirmed **methane (about 4 sigma)**. It found "no statistically significant or reliable evidence" for CO2 or DMS. It also concluded that an oxygen-poor mini-Neptune with no ocean and no life could explain the composition.
- **The full 0.6–12 µm spectrum:** A joint analysis in *Astronomy & Astrophysics* found "insufficient evidence" for DMS or DMDS. Other molecules containing methyl groups, such as ethane, fit equally well. The authors estimated that about **25 more MIRI transits** would be needed to settle the mid-infrared question at 3 sigma.
- **Standards of evidence:** A paper in *The Astronomical Journal* found that the MIRI spectrum is very sensitive to how the data are binned. Under the authors' preferred binning, 87.5% of retrievals did not favor DMS/DMDS. They attributed the apparent features to instrumental "red noise" and concluded the planet "does not meet the standards of evidence for life".

## Verdict: Disputed

What is fairly solid is that K2-18b's atmosphere contains methane. Whether it contains DMS or DMDS has not been established. Independent analyses largely attribute the signal to modeling choices and instrument systematics. Even a confirmed detection would not prove life, because abiotic routes to these molecules are still being studied. Whether K2-18b has an ocean at all is also uncertain. This page reflects published work we could verify as of October 2026. New JWST observations could change the picture, and we will update this page if they do.
