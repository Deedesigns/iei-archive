## The visitor

On October 19, 2017, the Pan-STARRS1 telescope in Hawaii discovered an object moving too fast to be bound to the Sun. Named **1I/'Oumuamua**, a Hawaiian word roughly meaning "scout", it was the first interstellar object ever observed passing through our solar system.

## What made it strange

![Artist's impression of a dark, reddish, elongated object in space](oumuamua-artist.jpg)

Kornmesser, via [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Artist%E2%80%99s_impression_of_the_interstellar_asteroid_%60Oumuamua_%28eso1820a%29.tiff), CC BY 4.0.*

- Its brightness varied dramatically as it tumbled, implying a very elongated or flat shape.
- No coma or tail of gas and dust was seen, unlike a typical comet.
- It sped up slightly as it left, beyond what the Sun's gravity alone explains, a *non-gravitational acceleration* reported in Nature in 2018.

## The claim

In 2018 Shmuel Bialy and Avi Loeb of Harvard proposed that the acceleration could come from sunlight pushing on a thin artificial lightsail. Loeb later expanded the idea in his 2021 book *Extraterrestrial*.

## The scientific response

Most astronomers disagreed. A 2019 review by the 'Oumuamua ISSI team in Nature Astronomy concluded that the observations were consistent with a natural origin. In 2023, Jennifer Bergner and Darryl Seligman published a paper in Nature showing that hydrogen gas trapped in a water-ice body and released as it warmed could produce the acceleration without a visible tail.

## Verdict

**Disputed.** No evidence requires an artificial origin, and natural explanations are favoured, but 'Oumuamua left before it could be studied closely, so the debate cannot be fully settled.
