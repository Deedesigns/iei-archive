## A strange green rock on the ice

![The Martian meteorite ALH84001, a fist-sized grayish-brown rock partly covered in dark fusion crust, photographed next to a 1 cm cube for scale](alh84001-meteorite.jpg)
*ALH84001 in the NASA Johnson Space Center curation lab, with a 1 cm cube for scale. Credit: NASA/JSC, via [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ALH84001.jpg), public domain.*

On **December 27, 1984**, Roberta Score of the U.S. Antarctic Search for Meteorites program (ANSMET) picked up a **1,931-gram** stone on the Far Western Icefield of the Allan Hills. NASA's *Martian Meteorite Compendium* quotes the field notes: a "highly-shocked, grayish-green, achondrite, 90% covered with fusion crust," with the added comment "Yowza-Yowza." It was the first meteorite processed from that season, so it got the label **ALH84001**.

For almost a decade it was filed as an ordinary asteroid meteorite (a diogenite). Then, in **1993**, odd chemistry (oxidized iron in its chromite, pyrite instead of troilite) and oxygen-isotope measurements showed it was actually from **Mars**. It is also unusually old. The compendium gives a formation age of roughly **4.5 to 4.0 billion years**, which makes it a piece of Mars's early crust from a time when the planet probably had liquid water.

Its travel history comes from radioactive and cosmic-ray clocks. Cosmic-ray exposure ages cluster around **14 to 18 million years**, meaning that is how long it drifted through space after an impact knocked it off Mars. Carbon-14 measurements put its fall to Earth at about **13,000 years ago**, after earlier estimates of 6,500 and 11,300 years were revised.

## The 1996 claim

In **August 1996**, David S. McKay of NASA Johnson Space Center and eight co-authors published "Search for Past Life on Mars: Possible Relic Biogenic Activity in Martian Meteorite ALH84001" in *Science* (vol. 273, pp. 924–930). NASA announced the result on **August 7, 1996**, and that afternoon **President Bill Clinton** spoke about it on the White House South Lawn: "Today, rock 84001 speaks to us across all those billions of years and millions of miles. It speaks of the possibility of life." He was careful to add that the finding "must be confirmed by other scientists."

The paper rested on four features found together in tiny orange **carbonate globules** inside cracks in the rock:

- **Organic molecules.** Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) on fresh fracture surfaces, which contamination tests suggested were native to the meteorite.
- **The carbonate globules themselves**, which the authors said resembled carbonates that bacteria help precipitate on Earth.
- **Magnetite and iron-sulfide grains.** Fine single-domain magnetite crystals, similar in shape and size to those made by magnetotactic bacteria on Earth.
- **"Microfossil" shapes.** Elongated and egg-shaped structures seen with a scanning electron microscope.

![Scanning electron microscope image of the ALH84001 surface showing a segmented, worm-like elongated structure lying across a lumpy mineral surface](alh84001-structures.jpg)
*The famous worm-like structure in ALH84001 seen with a scanning electron microscope. Some structures in the meteorite are only about 20 to 100 nanometers across. Credit: NASA/JSC, via [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ALH84001_structures.jpg), public domain.*

It's worth reading the paper's own wording. The abstract says "**Although inorganic formation is possible**," biological processes "could explain many of the observed features," and the features "could thus be fossil remains of a past martian biota." That is a hedged claim, not a declaration of discovery. The headlines were much less careful.

## How each line of evidence held up

The paper triggered one of the most intense scientific checks in planetary science. Here is where each strand landed.

- **The "fossils" are too small and shape is not proof.** Many of the shapes are smaller than any known free-living cell and too small to hold the machinery cells need. Researchers also stress that shape alone is a notoriously unreliable sign of life, because minerals can grow into bacteria-like forms.
- **The carbonates formed in cool water, but not necessarily with life.** In **2011**, Halevy, Fischer and Eiler used clumped-isotope measurements to show the carbonates formed at about **18 ± 4 °C** in a near-surface watery setting on Mars (*PNAS*). That is a habitable temperature, which is exciting, but it says nothing about whether anything lived there.
- **The magnetite can be made without biology.** In **2001**, D. C. Golden and colleagues at NASA JSC reported "a simple inorganic process" that produced carbonate globules with magnetite and sulfides like those in ALH84001 (*American Mineralogist*). In 2003, Allan Treiman argued in *Astrobiology* that the magnetite and carbon compounds are best explained by shock and heating during impacts. McKay's team replied that lab recipes used unrealistically pure ingredients and did not reproduce every feature at once.
- **The organics are real, and Martian, and abiotic.** A **2020** study by Koike and colleagues in *Nature Communications* found nitrogen-bearing organic matter preserved inside the carbonates, which confirms that organics formed on Mars. Then in **January 2022**, Andrew Steele and colleagues published nanoscale mapping in *Science* showing the organic material formed through **serpentinization and carbonation**, chemical reactions between hot water and basalt rock. Their conclusion: abiotic (non-biological) organic synthesis was happening on Mars about 4 billion years ago.

McKay and some colleagues continued to argue for a biological origin, including in a 2009–2010 follow-up that reported more "biomorphs." But that view did not win over most researchers.

## Claims vs. evidence at a glance

- **Claim: NASA found fossil life on Mars.** *Evidence:* NASA scientists proposed it as a possibility, in hedged language, in 1996. It was never confirmed.
- **Claim: The meteorite is really from Mars.** *Evidence:* Confirmed. Its oxygen isotopes and other chemistry match the Martian meteorite family.
- **Claim: The organic molecules show life.** *Evidence:* The organics are native to Mars, but the 2022 *Science* study ties them to water-rock chemistry, not biology.
- **Claim: The magnetite crystals could only come from bacteria.** *Evidence:* Lab experiments and shock-heating models produced similar magnetite without life.
- **Claim: The worm shapes are cells.** *Evidence:* Many are too small for known cells, and shape alone can't separate biology from mineral growth.

## Our verdict: Explained

ALH84001 is the best case so far of a serious, peer-reviewed claim of extraterrestrial life, made by real scientists with real data. All four of its pillars now have well-supported non-biological explanations, and the scientific community does not accept it as evidence of Martian life. One honest caveat is that no one has shown with certainty how *every* feature formed in one combined process, and the original authors never fully conceded. That leaves a small gap, but no positive evidence for life.

What the rock *did* prove is still remarkable. Early Mars had liquid water at about room temperature, and it was making organic molecules. ALH84001 helped launch modern astrobiology, and it's a big reason NASA's Perseverance rover is now collecting Martian rock samples, including possible biosignatures like [Cheyava Falls](cheyava-falls-mars-biosignature.html), that scientists hope to bring back to Earth labs. Those samples will face exactly the scrutiny ALH84001 did.
